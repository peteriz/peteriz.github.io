Weekly Training Card

Everything needed for each session, in one place.

Warm-up · 5 minutes Link to heading

Do these slowly before every NTC session. Progress by adding reps or hold time, never speed or range.

Curl-up — Lie on your back with hands palms-down under the lower back, one knee bent and one leg straight. Lift head and shoulders a few centimetres, keep the neck neutral, hold for 8 seconds, then lower. 5 reps. The lift is tiny; this is not a sit-up.

Side plank — Start on one elbow with knees bent and hips forward, making a straight line from ear to knee. Lift the hips and hold for 10 seconds. 3 per side. Straighten the legs when this becomes easy.

Bird dog — Start on all fours. Extend the opposite arm and leg to horizontal without tilting the hips, and hold for 8 seconds. 5 per side. Imagine balancing a cup of coffee on the lower back.

Best slot: morning before work or WFH midday. Backup: after the kids are asleep. Any day may slide by one day.

If a session is too hard: the warm-up plus half of the NTC circuit still counts. Build toward the full circuit over 3–4 weeks.

If your legs are sore: soreness after a new workout normally eases with each repeat. Keep Session B unchanged for 3–4 weeks. Seek medical advice if soreness lasts more than about five days or comes with marked swelling or dark urine.

Always substitute: crunch or sit-up → dead bug · V-up or leg raise → small reverse crunch · jump → low-impact option · sharp back pain → skip the move.

Tracking: start NTC sessions from the NTC app so they sync to Health. Track walks with Apple Watch → Workout → Outdoor Walk. Walking pace: full sentences are possible, singing is not, roughly 105–125 bpm.

Workday reset · twice daily Link to heading

Chin tucks ×10 → doorway chest stretch for 30 seconds → upper-trap stretch for 20 seconds per side → stand and do cat-cow ×5.

Getting out of bed: log-roll — roll onto your side as one unit, move the legs off the bed, then push up with the arms.

Safety Link to heading

  • Soreness or stiffness that eases with movement is normal. Sharp pain means skipping that move.
  • If a session is missed, skip it—never double up.
  • See a doctor if pain runs below the knee, there is numbness or tingling, pain occurs at night, or there is no improvement by about Week 6.
  • Stop immediately for chest pressure, unusual breathlessness, or dizziness.
